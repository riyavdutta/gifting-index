Method

What this can and can’t tell you

The data

Daily human pageviews (bots excluded) of each brand’s English Wikipedia article, loaded live from the Wikimedia API every time you open this page. The data runs one day behind.

Coach’s article only exists under its current name, “Coach New York”, from April 2018, so its history is shorter. Diptyque’s article was missing or renamed from March to August 2020; that stretch is left out rather than counted as zero.

What it measures

Curiosity, not purchase intent. People open a Wikipedia article to find out who a brand is, which makes it a good signal for news, controversy and gift research. It’s a weak signal for people who already know the brand and go straight to its shop.

It’s also English-language only, so it under-counts Charles & Keith’s home markets in Asia.

What didn’t hold up

An early version used monthly averages and found a March bump for Jo Malone, which looked like Mother’s Day in the UK. Switching to medians made it disappear: a few one-off spikes had been pulling the average up. The December pattern survived both methods, so it’s the one reported here.
